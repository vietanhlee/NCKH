\begin{abstract}
This data article presents \textbf{IC4SD-TrafficSnap}, a multimodal dataset coupling city-scale surveillance camera image time-series and geospatial road network mapping across \textbf{608} traffic surveillance stations in the urban and peri-urban corridors of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. The visual corpus contains \textbf{714,123} discrete camera snapshots (\textbf{44.38~GiB} / \textbf{47.66~GB} in JPEG format) captured over \textbf{93.6 continuous hours} (October 2--6, 2026). Across active acquisition sessions ($\Delta T < 3,600$~s), the pairwise inter-snapshot interval averages $\mathbf{269.0 \pm 239.7}$~s (median: $263.0$~s, $88.4\% \le 300$~s, $p_{90} \approx 312.0$~s), corresponding to a station-wise wall-clock interval of $286.9$~s/frame and a fleet-wide aggregate throughput of $0.47$~s/frame across all 608 stations. Physical shortest-path driving distances and directions are extracted from OpenStreetMap using the Open Source Routing Machine (OSRM v5.27.1). From these geospatial road assets, a $608 \times 608$ directed spatial graph containing \textbf{2,450} valid corridor links within a \textbf{6.0~km} search cutoff horizon is derived as a physical road connectivity representation, capturing directional asymmetry (\textbf{1,070} unidirectional links and \textbf{238} distance-asymmetric bidirectional pairs, alongside \textbf{452} metric-symmetric pairs). PyTorch ingestion toolchains and dual-transition directed diffusion operators are provided. Privacy assessments combining automated detector pre-screening across the full census and targeted multi-rater manual inspection of $1,000$ worst-case daylight frames confirmed zero readable license plates or identifiable facial biometrics, establishing a $95\%$ confidence upper bound of $p \le 0.30\%$ on that targeted sample via the Rule of Three. The dataset is released under a three-tier licensing framework: CC BY-NC 4.0 for imagery, ODbL for the road graph, and MIT for software utilities.
\end{abstract}

\section{Value of the Data}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Heterogeneous Mixed Urban Traffic Representation:} Captures multi-day visual dynamics of a Southeast Asian megacity where two-wheeled motorized vehicles account for \textbf{70--80\%} of modal traffic volume~\cite{jica2014hcmc}. This fills a critical empirical gap in intelligent transportation benchmarks, supporting macroscopic traffic state analysis and scene representation under dense mutual occlusions and non-lane-based movement patterns.
    \item \textbf{Macroscopic Spatial-Temporal Vision vs. Microscopic Tracking:} Native $512 \times 288$ resolution is deliberately matched with a 5-minute sampling cadence to enable city-scale macroscopic traffic state estimation, roadway background decomposition, and self-supervised visual representation pre-training (ViT, DINOv2, MAE) across 608 endpoints without prohibitive network bandwidth overhead or privacy risks.
    \item \textbf{Geospatial Road Network and Derived Directed Graph Topology:} Integrates surveillance visual streams with OpenStreetMap coordinates and shortest-path driving distance matrices ($608 \times 608$, \textbf{2,450} valid corridors). The directed spatial graph serves as a derived topological representation of physical road infrastructure connectivity (featuring 1,070 unidirectional links and 238 distance-asymmetric bidirectional pairs, alongside 452 metric-symmetric pairs; network tortuosity $\tau = 1.25 \pm 0.61$), providing a formal graph-theoretic substrate for structural network analysis, spatial corridor modeling, and geometric deep learning on urban transportation infrastructure.
    \item \textbf{Roadway Background and Incident Modeling:} The fixed-viewpoint multi-camera time-series across circadian day-night cycles provides an empirical basis for separating static roadway backgrounds from transient vehicular flow, enabling unsupervised anomaly detection, flood monitoring, and environmental scene decomposition.
    \item \textbf{Open Reproducibility and Three-Tier Licensing:} Accompanied by verified PyTorch data loaders, multi-camera batch samplers, and dual-transition directed graph processing routines. The dataset operates under a transparent three-tier licensing framework: visual surveillance imagery is released under \textit{Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 (CC BY-NC 4.0)} for academic research, the derived road network graph is released under the \textit{Open Database License (ODbL)}, and software utilities are distributed under the \textit{MIT License}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Background}
Large-scale spatial-temporal traffic benchmarks are foundational for developing intelligent transportation algorithms. However, established benchmarks in literature diverge into three disconnected paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Longitudinal 1D sensor-based traffic datasets} (such as METR-LA~\cite{li2018diffusion}, PEMS-BAY~\cite{li2018diffusion}, and LargeST~\cite{liu2023largest}) provide extensive graph topologies and longitudinal 1D time-series, but lack visual context, preventing models from leveraging rich photometric, meteorological, and vehicle-mix features.
    \item \textbf{Ego-vehicle driving benchmarks} (e.g., BDD100K~\cite{yu2020bdd100k}) provide rich visual sequences from moving dashcams, but capture singular mobile viewpoints lacking static city-scale surveillance infrastructure and continuous multi-station network topologies.
    \item \textbf{Localized computer vision surveillance benchmarks} (e.g., UA-DETRAC~\cite{wen2020ua}, CityFlow~\cite{tang2019cityflow}, MIO-TCD~\cite{luo2018miotcd}, and CityCam~\cite{zhang2017understanding}) offer multi-camera visual imagery, but either focus on microscopic vehicle tracking across a handful of isolated intersections (24–40 cameras) or provide static disconnected image crops lacking continuous time-series and formal city-scale geospatial routing tensors. Furthermore, they predominantly reflect car-dominant traffic in temperate North American or East Asian settings.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{IC4SD-TrafficSnap} bridges this fundamental division by providing an integrated multimodal dataset: continuous surveillance camera snapshots and geospatial road network mapping across \textbf{608} camera stations in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. From these primary road mapping and spatial assets, a directed spatial graph topology is established as a direct derived representation to support spatial graph representation learning and macroscopic transportation analysis. A systematic comparison with existing benchmarks is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:dataset_comparison}.

\subsection{Visual Snapshot Modality and Temporal Sampling}
The visual corpus consists of discrete 3-channel RGB JPEG snapshots (JFIF 1.01 standard) with a uniform native frame resolution of $\mathbf{512 \times 288}$ pixels (standard stream compression quality factor $\approx 75$--$80$). Each image file adheres to a deterministic, machine-readable filename schema:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Filename} = \langle\texttt{station\_id}\rangle \mathbin{\_} \langle\texttt{unix\_timestamp}\rangle\texttt{.jpg}
\end{equation}
where $\langle\texttt{station\_id}\rangle$ represents the historical integer station identifier ($1 \le \texttt{station\_id} \le 657$, indexing the 608 active stations cataloged across the municipal network and preserved from the initial pilot benchmark \textit{IC4SD-Traffic-HCM} v1.0), $\mathbin{\_}$ is an underscore delimiter, and $\langle\texttt{unix\_timestamp}\rangle$ represents the 10-digit integer UTC epoch seconds recorded at client reception (Vietnam Standard Time is $\text{UTC} + 7$). 

\paragraph{Tripartite Station Identifier Mapping Architecture}
To ensure persistent interoperability across raw image filenames, graph matrices, and municipal open data infrastructure, each surveillance endpoint is cross-referenced through a unified tripartite schema:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Matrix Index ($0 \le \texttt{matrix\_index} \le 607$):} A dense 0-indexed integer defining the row and column coordinate of the station in the $608 \times 608$ spatial graph adjacency and distance tensors (\path{graph/distance_km.npy}, \path{graph/direction.npy}).
    \item \textbf{Historical Station Identifier ($1 \le \texttt{station\_id} \le 657$):} The legacy station identifier (\texttt{stt}) retained from the initial pilot benchmark (\textit{IC4SD-Traffic-HCM} v1.0) and embedded directly into image filenames ($\langle\texttt{station\_id}\rangle\mathbin{\_}\langle\texttt{timestamp}\rangle\texttt{.jpg}$). Exactly 608 active endpoints are indexed across the range $[1, 657]$, with legacy gaps corresponding to inactive or decommissioned municipal feeds.
    \item \textbf{Municipal Technical Identifier (\texttt{CamID}):} The canonical 24-character hexadecimal camera ID assigned by the Ho Chi Minh City Department of Transportation streaming registry (\url{https://giaothong.hochiminhcity.gov.vn}), ensuring persistent traceability to official municipal camera metadata.
\end{enumerate}
A complete cross-reference lookup table synchronizing \texttt{matrix\_index}, \texttt{station\_id}, \texttt{CamID}, arterial locations, and geographic coordinates is archived in \path{metadata/routes.csv} and \path{metadata/stations.csv}. Exactly \textbf{608 camera stations} are indexed in the dataset across the municipal road network.

Snapshots were acquired across \textbf{93.6 continuous hours} spanning 5 calendar days (from October 2, 2026, 17:37 ICT to October 6, 2026, 15:12 ICT). The empirical inter-snapshot acquisition dynamics exhibit two distinct statistical perspectives:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Active Pairwise Sampling Distribution ($\Delta T < 3,600$~s):} Evaluated strictly across consecutive snapshot pairs within active polling sessions without prolonged disconnections, the inter-snapshot interval averages $\mathbf{\Delta T = 269.0 \pm 239.7}$~s (median: $263.0$~s, mode: $240$--$270$~s, $88.4\% \le 300$~s, $p_{90} \approx 312.0$~s, $p_{95} \approx 520.0$~s, $p_{99} \approx 1,200.0$~s; nominal target: $300$~s / 5.0 minutes). The standard deviation arises from practical IoT network dynamics and upstream transcoder schedules: the municipal streaming server refreshes its internal HLS frame buffer at intervals of approximately $240$--$270$~s, and client asynchronous polling with cache-busting headers retrieves the newly available frame as soon as rendered, explaining why the median interval ($263.0$~s) is faster than the nominal $300$~s target. The heavy right tail is driven by distributed HTTP timeout events, cellular/fiber packet drops, and upstream streaming transcoder delays across all 608 stations.
    \item \textbf{Global Unfiltered Interval Distribution and Fleet Cadence:} When evaluated across the complete unfiltered sequence including transmission interruptions, inter-snapshot intervals range from $2.0$~s to an empirical maximum of $4.12$~hours ($14,832$~s, recorded at a single peri-urban endpoint during a sustained network outage). Across the entire 93.6-hour observation window, the station-wise wall-clock interval averages $93.6\text{h} \times 3,600\text{s} / 1,174.5 \approx \mathbf{286.9}$~s/frame/station across the fleet, with the $17.9$~s discrepancy from active sampling directly reflecting aggregate telemetry packet dropouts. In contrast, at the fleet level, the centralized ingestion server achieved an aggregate throughput of $93.6\text{h} \times 3,600\text{s} / 714,123 \approx \mathbf{0.47}$~s/frame ($\approx 2.12$ frames/s received across all 608 stations).
\end{enumerate}
An empirical analysis of inter-snapshot spacing indicates that $\mathbf{88.4\%}$ of intervals satisfy $\Delta T \le 300$~s, $\mathbf{7.8\%}$ fall between $300$ and $600$~s, and only $\mathbf{3.8\%}$ exceed $600$~s. For spatio-temporal deep learning architectures that require regular temporal grids (\path{code/camera_sampler.py}), snapshots are quantized onto fixed discrete 5-minute intervals ($\tau = 300$~s) via nearest-neighbor binning when the alignment deviation is bounded within $\pm 2.5$~minutes ($\le 150$~s); for larger gaps exceeding $300$~s ($11.6\%$ of intervals), temporal missing masks (`mask = 0`) are assigned rather than duplicating stale frames.

The total archive contains \textbf{714,123 valid surveillance snapshots} (\textbf{44.38~GiB} binary volume, equivalent to \textbf{47.66~GB} decimal). Across the \textbf{608 camera stations}, the dataset delivers a dense, multi-camera visual corpus averaging $\mathbf{1,174.5 \pm 235.1}$ snapshots per station. The standard deviation of $235.1$ snapshots around the mean of $1,174.5$ frames is directly attributable to heterogeneous transmission conditions across the municipal IoT infrastructure: while $84.2\%$ of endpoints exhibited high availability, outlying cellular links experienced occasional packet drops, yielding a distinctly tiered reliability distribution. Relative to the theoretical nominal target of $1,123$ frames (derived from 93.6~hours at regular $300$~s polling), stations are categorized into three operational continuity tiers:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{High Operational Continuity (512 stations, 84.2\%):} Stations with $\ge 1,100$ snapshots ($\ge 98\%$ of nominal baseline), reaching up to $1,268$ frames at stations benefiting from rapid $240$--$270$~s buffer refresh cycles.
    \item \textbf{Moderate Operational Continuity (82 stations, 13.5\%):} Stations recording $600$--$1,099$ snapshots ($53\%$--$98\%$ of nominal baseline) subject to intermittent cellular telemetry packet loss.
    \item \textbf{Intermittent / Peripheral Continuity (14 stations, 2.3\%):} Stations recording $<600$ snapshots ($<53\%$ of nominal baseline) situated in outlying peri-urban districts subject to extended transmission disruptions.
\end{itemize}
All 608 stations remain geodetically cataloged and topologically mapped in the accompanying spatial graph.

Temporal partitioning based on standard clock hours (Daytime: 06:00--18:00 ICT; Nighttime: 18:00--06:00 ICT) yields \textbf{348,464 daytime frames} ($48.8\%$) and \textbf{365,659 nighttime frames} ($51.2\%$). Because data collection commenced late in the afternoon (17:37) on Day 1 and concluded mid-afternoon (15:12) on Day 5, afternoon time slots span 5 calendar days while late-night intervals span 4 cycles. An empirical audit of on-screen overlay timestamps across 500 sampled daylight frames indicates that client ingestion introduces an average offset of $\mathbf{\Delta t_{\text{lag}} = 15.0 \pm 4.2}$~s relative to camera hardware clocks on NTP-synchronized stations. This reflects the elapsed duration between frame rendering by the municipal HLS streaming transcoder and client socket reception. Crucially, the audit revealed that approximately $1.8\%$ of camera endpoints (11 stations) exhibit de-synchronized internal hardware clocks (displaying historical factory default times due to lack of local NTP synchronization). Consequently, the client-recorded UTC epoch timestamp embedded in each file name serves as the canonical, synchronized temporal index. The spatial distribution of camera stations and diurnal photometric properties are presented in Figures~\ref{fig:spatial_map} and \ref{fig:temporal_photometric}. A quantitative breakdown of the visual snapshot corpus is provided in Table~\ref{tab:summary_stats}.

\subsection{Geospatial Road Network and Derived Directed Spatial Graph Topology}
The structural substrate of the dataset is defined by the physical roadway network of Ho Chi Minh City. Comprehensive geospatial metadata is compiled in \path{metadata/routes.csv} (recording station IDs, geographic coordinates, administrative localities, arterial classifications, and camera mounting elevations across all \textbf{608} camera locations) alongside the pairwise shortest driving distance matrix \path{metadata/road_network_distance.csv}.

Rather than treating the spatial graph as an abstract primitive, a directed spatial graph topology is established as a direct \textbf{derived mathematical representation} synthesized from these geospatial road coordinates and OpenStreetMap routing paths. Machine-learning tensors (\path{graph/distance_km.npy}, \path{graph/direction.npy}, and \path{graph/edges.csv}) are structured as $608 \times 608$ arrays for direct ingestion into spatio-temporal graph neural networks. Entry $d_{ij}$ denotes the shortest physical driving distance along navigable OpenStreetMap road links from camera station $i$ to station $j$.

\textbf{Corridor Adjacency Construction Rule:} An unconstrained geometric radius graph within $6.0$~km would yield over $190,000$ links due to dense urban camera spacing in central districts. Instead, the road network graph models sequential arterial corridor connectivity via a rigorous three-stage algorithmic pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Geometric \& Navigability Search:} Pairwise driving routes between all 608 stations are evaluated via OSRM within a spatial bounding cutoff horizon of $\kappa = \mathbf{6.0}$~km;
    \item \textbf{Immediate Downstream Corridor Pruning:} A candidate directed edge $(i, j)$ is retained if and only if station $j$ represents an immediate downstream surveillance node along the roadway without intervening intermediate cameras. An intermediate camera is defined as any third camera $k$ located within an orthogonal buffer of $50$~m from the traversed driving path with $d_{ik} < d_{ij}$;
    \item \textbf{Directionality Constraints:} Vehicular movement must be legally permissible along the corridor under OpenStreetMap road directionality attributes (\texttt{oneway} tags).
\end{enumerate}
Diagonal entries ($i = j$) denote regularized self-distances ($d_{ii} = 0.0$ in numeric arrays), and non-connected pairs are represented by $\text{NaN}$.

Under this three-stage sequential corridor adjacency rule, the derived road graph contains exactly \textbf{2,450 valid directed edges} across \textbf{1,760 connected node pairs} (network sparsity: \textbf{99.34\%}, density: \textbf{0.66\%}):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Unidirectional Corridor Links (No Direct Reverse Edge):} \textbf{1,070 node pairs} (\textbf{60.8\%} of connected topology) possess directed connectivity in only one travel direction. This structural asymmetry arises from two distinct mechanisms: (i) designated one-way municipal traffic regulations on arterial pairs (verified against OpenStreetMap \texttt{oneway=yes} tags); and (ii) algorithmic corridor pruning asymmetry, wherein the reverse travel path between $j$ and $i$ passes through intermediate junction cameras located along the return carriageway, thereby decomposing the return route into multiple shorter links and leaving no direct $(j, i)$ single-hop corridor link.
    \item \textbf{Bidirectional Corridor Pairs:} \textbf{690 node pairs} (\textbf{39.2\%} of connected topology) are mutually accessible in both traffic directions within $6.0$~km.
    \item \textbf{Corridor Distance Asymmetry Taxonomy:} Among the 690 bidirectional pairs, \textbf{452 pairs} (\textbf{65.51\%} of bidirectional topology, or \textbf{25.68\%} of all 1,760 connected pairs) exhibit metric symmetry ($|d_{ij} - d_{ji}| < 50$~m), whereas \textbf{238 pairs} (\textbf{34.49\%} of bidirectional topology, or \textbf{13.52\%} of all 1,760 connected pairs) exhibit significant distance asymmetry ($|d_{ij} - d_{ji}| \ge 50$~m), directly induced by physical median barriers, grade-separated flyovers, and compulsory U-turn routing on divided dual-carriageway boulevards. From a technical and computational standpoint, evaluating asymmetry on the discrete integer-meter edge list (\path{edges.csv}) reveals 231 pairs with $|d_{ij} - d_{ji}| > 50.0$~m and 243 pairs with $|d_{ij} - d_{ji}| \ge 50.0$~m (accounting for exactly 12 pairs exhibiting an identical 50.0~m median traversal discrepancy); when evaluated on the canonical GNN continuous floating-point kilometer distance matrix (\path{distance_km.npy}), exactly 238 pairs satisfy $|d_{ij} - d_{ji}| \ge 50.0$~m. The mean directional discrepancy across bidirectional corridors is $\mathbf{115.9 \pm 266.2}$~m (median: $24.0$~m), reaching a maximum divergence of $\mathbf{2,780.0}$~m ($2.78$~km).
    \item \textbf{Topological Node Roles and Connectivity:} Structural degree analysis identifies \textbf{598} regular interconnected stations, \textbf{5} sink stations (in-degree $>0$, out-degree $=0$: Stations 212, 284, 443, 446, 516), \textbf{3} source stations (in-degree $=0$, out-degree $>0$: Stations 215, 498, 557), and \textbf{2} peripheral stations situated beyond $6.0$~km from all others (in-degree $=0$, out-degree $=0$: Stations 141, 489). The graph contains \textbf{6 weakly connected components} (dominated by a giant component of 599 nodes, 98.5\% of the network, alongside five small components of sizes 3, 2, 2, 1, 1) and \textbf{19 strongly connected components}. Valid edge routing distances span from $3.0$~m to $6,000.0$~m (mean: $1,159.6 \pm 1,088.9$~m; median: $823.0$~m; interquartile range: $[384.0, 1,560.0]$~m; $90^{\text{th}}$ percentile: $2,681.0$~m). The minimal routing distances (e.g., $3.0$~m) represent co-located directional surveillance cameras mounted on the same physical gantry or mast monitoring orthogonal approach directions at complex multi-way intersections.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Road Network Circuitousness and Spatial Density:} To quantify the geometric deviation of navigable urban driving routes relative to Euclidean straight-line geodesic distance, the network tortuosity index (circuitousness ratio $\tau_{ij} = d_{ij} / d_{ij}^{\text{geodesic}}$) was evaluated across all connected corridors separating stations by $\ge 100$~m ($N = 2,264$). The empirical tortuosity yields an overall mean of $\mathbf{1.25 \pm 0.61}$ (median: $\mathbf{1.12}$, interquartile range: $[1.01, 1.33]$, $90^{\text{th}}$ percentile: $1.64$). This physical tortuosity index encapsulates the distinct riparian delta geography of Ho Chi Minh City, where the urban fabric is heavily bisected by the meandering Saigon River and an intricate network of tidal waterways and drainage canals (e.g., Nhieu Loc--Thi Nghe, Tau Hu--Ben Nghe, Kenh Doi, and Kenh Te). Arterial traffic is channeled through bottleneck bridges, elevated flyovers, and compulsory U-turn turnarounds around continuous concrete median barriers, causing actual physical driving routes to be on average $25\%$ longer than Euclidean straight lines, with $10\%$ of corridors deviating by more than $64\%$ ($\tau \ge 1.64$). Furthermore, spatial station density analysis indicates a nearest-neighbor Euclidean distance between adjacent camera installations averaging $\mathbf{328.7 \pm 605.5}$~m (median: $\mathbf{170.3}$~m, interquartile range: $[69.6, 339.7]$~m, minimum: $2.7$~m on shared intersection gantries, maximum: $10.15$~km at peripheral exurban stations). Stations span a comprehensive geographic bounding box $[10.642472^\circ\text{N} - 10.988295^\circ\text{N}, 106.452713^\circ\text{E} - 106.850624^\circ\text{E}]$, encompassing both historical core districts and expanding peri-urban transport arteries.

\section{Experimental Design, Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Surveillance Infrastructure and Asynchronous Ingestion Pipeline}
The visual data source is the public municipal traffic surveillance web portal maintained by the Ho Chi Minh City Department of Transportation (\url{https://giaothong.hochiminhcity.gov.vn}). Geographic coordinates, intersection approaches, and camera mounting elevations (ranging from $\mathbf{6.0}$ to $\mathbf{15.0}$~meters above street level, as cataloged in the \texttt{camera\_elevation\_m} attribute of \path{routes.csv}) are obtained from official municipal technical metadata registry and cross-verified against OpenStreetMap road geometry and ground observations. Cameras are mounted on elevated steel gantries and traffic signal masts oriented at downward oblique angles ($15^\circ - 40^\circ$) to monitor arterial queues and intersection approaches.

To ingest snapshots across \textbf{608 concurrent camera endpoints} without inducing network bottlenecks or file corruption, an asynchronous Python ingestion pipeline was deployed:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{HTTP/1.1 Connection Pooling:} Persistent sessions with Keep-Alive connection pooling and automatic exponential backoff retries (maximum $3$ retries, backoff factor $0.3$) managed through \texttt{urllib3}.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Offset Ingestion Timer:} Thread execution latency is tracked during each cycle to adjust polling offsets, targeting nominal $300$~s inter-snapshot spacing across the fleet.
    \item \textbf{Atomic File Serialization:} Retrieved JPEG streams are decoded in memory (\texttt{cv2.imdecode}) and verified before being written to temporary files and committed via atomic OS renames (\texttt{os.replace}), preventing truncated or zero-byte files on disk.
    \item \textbf{Automated Artifact Removal:} Municipal transmission interruptions occasionally yield standardized error placeholder cards ($284 \times 177$ pixels, payload size $5.8$--$7.2$~KB). The ingestion pipeline automatically identifies and discards these placeholder cards, ensuring valid traffic snapshots in the released archive (overall acquisition pass rate: \textbf{99.85\%}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Road Network Distance Extraction from OpenStreetMap}
Physical routing distances $d_{ij}$ and driving trajectories were computed using the Open Source Routing Machine (\textbf{OSRM v5.27.1}) engine operating on OpenStreetMap planet road network data extracted in \textbf{October 2026} (\copyright\ OpenStreetMap contributors, licensed under the Open Database License ODbL). Exact geographic coordinates (latitude, longitude) cataloged in \path{metadata/routes.csv} were mapped to the nearest navigable road centerline using OSRM's nearest-node projection, with a radial snapping error under $15.0$~meters across all stations. To guarantee permanent reproducibility independent of future modifications to the live OpenStreetMap database, the derived topological artifacts (\path{metadata/road_network_distance.csv} and \path{graph/edges.csv}) are archived as immutable, frozen static snapshots with verifiable SHA-256 checksums in the Zenodo repository, accompanied by an exported OSM road network boundary extract.

Because surveillance camera endpoints are predominantly deployed along primary municipal arterials, boulevards, and bridge approaches rather than narrow residential alleys, routing was calculated using the standardized motor-vehicle driving profile. This profile accurately captures urban traffic flow rules, median divider turn restrictions, grade-separated overpasses, and designated one-way circulation patterns across the metropolitan network. While mixed motorcycle traffic constitutes a dominant volume fraction in Ho Chi Minh City, both vehicular modes share identical directional corridors along primary arterials; informal motorcycle cut-throughs in residential alleyways fall outside the macroscopic coverage of the municipal camera network. Pairwise shortest driving routes were queried within a spatial bounding search horizon of $\kappa = \mathbf{6.0}$~km, from which the three-stage corridor pipeline extracted exactly \textbf{2,450 valid directed corridors}.

\subsection{Directed Graph Modeling Formulations}
To facilitate spatio-temporal graph learning on this directed asymmetric topology, two standard mathematical formulations are provided in \path{code/graph_utils.py}:

\subsubsection{Directed Dual-Transition Matrices (DCRNN / Graph WaveNet Formulation)}
For directed road graphs, modeling traffic propagation requires preserving directional asymmetry. Following the diffusion convolution framework of Li et al.~\cite{li2018diffusion}, the weighted adjacency matrix $W_{ij}$ is computed using a thresholded Gaussian Radial Basis Function (RBF) kernel evaluated on the valid directed corridor links:
\begin{equation}
    W_{ij} = \begin{cases} 
    \exp\left( -\frac{d_{ij}^2}{\sigma^2} \right), & \text{if } (i, j) \in E \\ 
    0, & \text{if } (i, j) \notin E 
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $E$ denotes the set of \textbf{2,450 valid directed edges}, and $\sigma = \mathbf{1.09}$~km is the empirical standard deviation of valid road distances. Computing $W$ strictly over pruned sequential corridors $E$ avoids spurious dense cross-connectivity while preserving physical routing topology. Under this formulation with $\sigma = 1.09$~km, proximal camera stations with $d_{ij} \le 1.0$~km retain significant connection weight ($W_{ij} \ge \exp(-(1.0/1.09)^2) \approx 0.43$), attenuating to $\approx 0.15$ at $1.5$~km and decaying toward negligible levels at larger separations ($\approx 0.005$ at $2.5$~km). Empirically, the valid edge distance distribution concentrates heavily within this active spatial interaction envelope: \textbf{799 edges (32.61\%)} represent immediate proximal links ($d_{ij} \le 500$~m), \textbf{1,445 edges (58.98\%)} lie within $1.0$~km, \textbf{1,799 edges (73.43\%)} fall within $1.5$~km, and \textbf{2,027 edges (82.73\%)} span $\le 2.0$~km, while only \textbf{192 edges (7.84\%)} represent long-distance inter-district corridors ($d_{ij} > 3.0$~km; $90^{\text{th}}$ percentile: $2,681.0$~m). This empirical spatial concentration is consistent with localized urban traffic interactions across consecutive signalized intersections; bounding spatial influence via kernel decay is recognized in graph representation learning literature~\cite{li2018diffusion,wu2019graph} to mitigate spatial over-smoothing in multi-layer spatio-temporal convolutions. The bounding threshold $\kappa = 6.0$~km serves as the initial geometric search envelope for corridor path discovery rather than an arbitrary RBF truncation cutoff. Directed random walk diffusion is governed by a forward transition matrix $P_f$ (downstream traffic flow) and a backward transition matrix $P_b$ (upstream sensory influence):
\begin{equation}
    P_f = D_O^{-1} W, \quad P_b = D_I^{-1} W^\top
\end{equation}
where $D_O = \text{diag}(\sum_{j} W_{ij})$ and $D_I = \text{diag}(\sum_{i} W_{ij})$ denote the out-degree and in-degree diagonal matrices, respectively. To handle sink, source, or isolated nodes ($D_{O, ii} = 0$ or $D_{I, ii} = 0$), regularized self-loops ($\tilde{W} = W + I_N$) or zero-degree masked normalizations are implemented in \path{code/graph_utils.py}, ensuring proper row-stochastic diffusion matrices with unit spectral radius $\rho(P_f) = 1.0$ and $\rho(P_b) = 1.0$. This mathematically ensures that the multi-hop diffusion operator is non-expansive and numerically stable, preventing unbounded signal amplification during multi-step graph convolutions.

\subsubsection{Symmetrized Normalized Laplacian (ChebNet / STGCN Formulation)}
For spectral graph convolutions that require a self-adjoint operator (e.g., ChebNet~\cite{defferrard2016convolutional}, STGCN~\cite{yu2018spatio}), the adjacency matrix is symmetrized as $W_{\text{sym}} = \frac{1}{2}(W + W^\top)$. The symmetric normalized Laplacian $L_{\text{sym}} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times N}$ is computed as~\cite{kipf2017semi}:
\begin{equation}
    L_{\text{sym}} = I_N - D_{\text{sym}}^{-1/2} W_{\text{sym}} D_{\text{sym}}^{-1/2}
\end{equation}
where $D_{\text{sym}} = \text{diag}(\sum_{j} W_{\text{sym}, ij})$. For isolated peripheral stations with zero degree ($D_{\text{sym}, ii} = 0$, such as Stations 141 and 489), regularized zero-diagonal scalings are assigned in \path{code/graph_utils.py} ($D_{\text{sym}, ii}^{-1/2} = 0$), preventing division-by-zero. 

By spectral graph theory~\cite{chung1997spectral}, the eigenvalues of the normalized Laplacian on any finite graph are mathematically bounded within $[0, 2]$. On the full 608-node graph of IC4SD-TrafficSnap, the presence of disconnected two-node components ($K_2$ subgraphs with symmetric edges) yields eigenvalues $\{0, 2.0\}$, establishing the global maximum eigenvalue as $\lambda_{\max}^{(\text{global})} = 2.0$. Consequently, to ensure unconditional numerical stability across all 608 nodes and prevent exponential divergence in Chebyshev polynomial recursions $T_k(\tilde{L})$, the rescaled Chebyshev Laplacian is defined using the theoretical global upper bound $\lambda_{\max} = 2.0$:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{L} = \frac{2}{\lambda_{\max}} L_{\text{sym}} - I_N = L_{\text{sym}} - I_N
\end{equation}
which guarantees that all eigenvalues of $\tilde{L}$ lie strictly within $[-1, 1]$. Users should note that when analyzing only the dominant giant connected component (599 stations), the empirical maximum eigenvalue evaluates to $\lambda_{\max}^{(\text{giant})} = 1.272$, reflecting high connectivity and non-bipartite graph density; however, applying $\lambda_{\max} = 2.0$ globally is mathematically necessary to avoid divergence on the peripheral smaller components. Symmetrization intentionally eliminates directional flow asymmetry, which users must balance against the need for an orthogonal eigenbasis.

\subsection{Empirical Image Quality and Photometric Audit}
Image quality descriptors were computed across the dataset to evaluate photometric diversity:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Perceived Luminance ($Y$):} Calculated via standard ITU-R BT.601 conversion $Y = 0.299R + 0.587G + 0.114B$. The empirical overall mean luminance is $\mathbf{98.23 \pm 16.35}$ (range: $[66.4, 126.8]$, Figure~\ref{fig:temporal_photometric}a). Nighttime luminance remains centered between $\mathbf{87}$ and $\mathbf{90}$ (Figure~\ref{fig:temporal_photometric}a) due to dense municipal LED and high-pressure sodium street lighting along urban arterials, combined with the automatic gain control (AGC) and exposure compensation of municipal cameras.
    \item \textbf{Root-Mean-Square (RMS) Contrast:} Evaluated as the pixel standard deviation across viewports, yielding $\mathbf{45.70}$, confirming substantial textural variation between asphalt roadways and vehicle clusters.
    \item \textbf{Shannon Spatial Entropy:} Histogram pixel entropy averages $\mathbf{7.28 \pm 0.33}$~bits (theoretical upper bound: $8.0$~bits), confirming high spatial information density without saturation clipping.
    \item \textbf{Laplacian Edge Sharpness:} Grayscale Laplacian variance $\text{Var}(\nabla^2 I)$ averages $\mathbf{3,015.71 \pm 1,499.86}$. An empirical reference baseline of $\text{Var}(\nabla^2 I) \ge 50$ was adopted as an engineering heuristic to flag severe optical defocus or stream degradation~\cite{pech2000diatom}. The substantial empirical mean of $\mathbf{3,015.71 \pm 1,499.86}$ reflects high scene detail from rich urban textures (vehicular clusters, road markings, roadside foliage) as well as standard JPEG compression block boundaries, confirming that camera viewports maintain usable focus.
    \item \textbf{Inter-Snapshot Dynamics:} Successive-frame pixel difference analysis across consecutive 5-minute sampling pairs was evaluated on 8-bit grayscale channels in floating-point precision ($\text{diff} = |I_t - I_{t-1}|$). The empirical Mean Absolute Difference (MAD) yields a median of $\mathbf{9.45}$ ($\mu = 10.82 \pm 3.65$, range: $[2.50, 32.40]$, interquartile range: $[7.20, 13.80]$), with an active pixel displacement ratio yielding a median of $\mathbf{15.82\%}$ ($\mu = 18.41 \pm 6.20\%$ of frame pixels satisfying $|I_t - I_{t-1}| > 15$). This moderate pixel variation corresponds directly to vehicular displacement and queue progression across traffic lanes, while static structural regions (buildings, sidewalks, road surfaces) remain invariant. During data ingestion, identical stream buffer frames ($\text{MAD} < 0.5$) resulting from upstream rendering latency were programmatically screened out. Across the archived operational time-series, the empirical 1st percentile of consecutive MAD is $\mathbf{2.80}$ gray levels (strictly within the $[2.50, 32.40]$ empirical distribution and well above the $0.5$ deduplication threshold), confirming continuous vehicular and environmental variations without static playback freezing.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Visual Privacy and Multi-Tiered Empirical Audit}
To rigorously evaluate alignment with personal data protection regulations in Vietnam (Decree 13/2023/ND-CP, Decree 47/2020/ND-CP, and Law 91/2025/QH15), a multi-tiered empirical privacy audit combining optical geometry analysis, automated candidate screening, and multi-rater manual inspection was conducted:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Optical Perspective and Ground Sampling Distance (GSD):} Surveillance cameras are installed on municipal overhead gantries and signal masts at heights between $\mathbf{6.0}$ and $\mathbf{15.0}$~m, oriented at downward oblique pitch angles ($15^\circ - 40^\circ$) to monitor macroscopic intersection queues. At native $512 \times 288$ pixel resolution, the nominal GSD across mid-road viewports ($25$--$35$~m standoff distance) measures $\mathbf{2.73 - 3.25}$~cm/pixel, deteriorating to $>5.0$~cm/pixel in the background ($>40$~m).
    \item \textbf{Empirical Optical Resolution Limits in Nominal and Foreground Lanes:} Standard Vietnamese motorcycle license plates measure $19.0 \times 14.0$~cm. At nominal mid-road GSD, a plate projects onto approximately $7 \times 5$ sensor pixels, with individual alphanumeric character strokes subtending under $2.0$ pixels ($\approx 1.79$~px) in stroke height. To analyze foreground geometry under worst-case optical resolution regimes, consider camera installations on overhead gantries and signal masts ($H \approx 6.0$--$8.0$~m, downward depression angle $\theta \approx 20^\circ$--$40^\circ$). Under nominal traffic lanes at ground standoff $D \approx 14$--$18$~m (slant range $R = \sqrt{H^2 + D^2} \approx 15$--$20$~m), or steep foreground intersection approach angles ($\theta \approx 35^\circ$--$40^\circ$ at $D \approx 7$--$10$~m), the ground sampling distance reaches its operational lower bound at $\text{GSD}_{\min} \approx 1.85$--$2.10$~cm/pixel. Here, a motorcycle plate projects to at most $\approx 9 \times 7$ pixels, with character stroke height measuring at most $\mathbf{2.5 - 2.8\text{ pixels}}$. This remains far below empirical optical character legibility standards for automatic license plate recognition (ALPR), which established literature indicates requires a stroke height of $\ge 16$--$20$ pixels for reliable optical character segmentation and identification~\cite{du2013automatic}. In addition, plate tilt angles, tropical thermal shimmer, motion blur from vehicular transit ($30$--$50$~km/h), and $8 \times 8$ lossy JPEG block compression render individual alphanumeric characters unresolvable.
    \item \textbf{Facial Anonymity and Commuting Occlusion:} Under Vietnamese National Road Traffic Law (Decree 100/2019/ND-CP), helmet usage is legally mandatory for all motorcycle operators, resulting in near-universal compliance in urban traffic streams. Furthermore, commuters overwhelmingly wear multi-layer protective fabric sun/dust masks and UV sunglasses against tropical heat and vehicle exhaust. Combined with steep downward camera pitch, facial biometric regions project onto fewer than $8 \times 8$ pixels, preventing biometric facial re-identification at the sensing layer.
    \item \textbf{Automated Candidate Screening and Low-Recall Limitations:} Automated screening across the entire corpus of \textbf{714,123 visual snapshots} was conducted using two specialized deep learning models: YOLOv8x-face (pretrained on WiderFace) for facial detection and YOLOv8x / LPRNet (pretrained on vehicle license plate benchmarks) for plate localization, operating at a low confidence threshold ($\tau = 0.25$) to maximize candidate generation. The detectors generated $1,420$ raw candidate bounding boxes, which upon inspection were all confirmed to be background texture false alarms (asphalt patches, motorcycle headlamps, specular reflections, roadside traffic signage). Zero readable license plates or identifiable faces were found. However, automated detectors inherently suffer from low recall on low-resolution imagery below the Nyquist limit, and zero automated detections alone do not constitute absolute proof of zero risk.
    \item \textbf{Targeted Boundary Stress Audit and Stratified Random Census Audit:} To overcome automated detector recall limits, human inspection was executed across two complementary sampling regimes by three independent reviewers:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textit{Worst-Case Boundary Stress Sample ($N_{\text{audit}} = 1,000$):} Selected strictly from the lowest-mounted cameras ($H \approx 6.0$~m, standoff distance $<20$~m) during peak midday sunlight ($11:00$--$13:00$ ICT), representing the maximal optical resolution regime for potential personal identification.
        \item \textit{Stratified Random Census Sample ($N_{\text{random}} = 2,000$):} Drawn uniformly across all 608 camera stations and 24 diurnal hours to ensure unbiased global representation across the entire time-series.
    \end{enumerate}
    Prior to evaluating the dataset, all three independent human raters underwent a positive control calibration protocol using a multi-tier synthetic degradation suite (50 baseline traffic scenes featuring verified ground-truth facial biometrics and readable license plates, synthetically downscaled to match the optical GSD range of $1.85$--$3.5$~cm/px across varying pitch angles and compression artifacts). The calibration confirmed that while raters achieved $100\%$ detection recall on uncompressed near-field controls, human identification recall dropped unanimously to $0\%$ once imagery fell below $\text{GSD} \ge 1.85$~cm/px (stroke height $<3$~px), confirming that anonymity is optically guaranteed by sensor sampling geometry rather than auditor false-negatives. During the blind audit of both dataset batches ($3,000$ total snapshots), the raters achieved complete unanimous agreement (\textbf{100\% inter-rater agreement}, $P_o = 1.0$), identifying \textbf{0 readable license plates} and \textbf{0 identifiable facial biometrics}. Applying the statistical Rule of Three~\cite{hanley1983if}, the $95\%$ confidence interval upper bound on residual privacy breach probability is bounded at:
    \begin{equation}
        p_{\text{worst}} \le \frac{3}{1,000} = 0.30\% \quad (3.0 \times 10^{-3}), \qquad p_{\text{random}} \le \frac{3}{2,000} = 0.15\% \quad (1.5 \times 10^{-3}).
    \end{equation}
    Crucially, privacy assurance does not depend on automated detectors alone, but is anchored upon a tripartite defense: (i) physical sub-Nyquist optics ($\text{GSD} \ge 1.85$~cm/px), (ii) statutory cultural occlusion (mandatory helmets and masks), and (iii) dual empirical audits with calibrated human verification.
    \item \textbf{Contextual Privacy Scope:} Urban contextual features such as pedestrian silhouettes, storefront signage, and public transit liveries are visible in macroscopic viewports; however, because snapshots are captured at discontinuous 5-minute sampling intervals, the dataset does not permit continuous individual tracking or trajectory reconstruction.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Technical Validation: Empirical Network Spatial-Temporal Dynamics and Topological Consistency}
\label{sec:technical_validation}
To substantiate the technical validity of the derived road network topology and verify its physical consistency with actual metropolitan vehicular movement, multi-faceted empirical validations were conducted:

\paragraph{Physical Infrastructure vs. Dynamic Flow Distinction}
Users should explicitly distinguish between static physical roadway infrastructure and dynamic traffic states: the derived $608 \times 608$ directed spatial graph models navigable physical roadway connectivity and shortest-path driving distances ($d_{ij}$) extracted via OSRM, capturing immutable physical infrastructure geometry rather than real-time dynamic vehicular flow.

\paragraph{Visual Motion Dynamics Coupling vs. Unconnected Control Null Model}
To examine whether connected OSRM corridors exhibit meaningful physical traffic coupling rather than arbitrary isotropic proximity, we evaluated the spatial-temporal correlation of visual dynamics signals across the network. To account for camera exposure variations, automatic gain control (AGC), and circadian daylight transitions, consecutive inter-frame motion dynamics signals ($\text{MAD}_i(t)$) were diurnal-detrended via hourly z-score normalization ($\widetilde{\text{MAD}}_i(t) = (\text{MAD}_i(t) - \mu_i(h(t))) / \sigma_i(h(t))$) and aligned onto regular 5-minute sampling grids. The Pearson correlation coefficient $r_{ij}$ was evaluated across all \textbf{2,450 valid directed corridors} partitioned across four exhaustive distance brackets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Immediate Proximal Corridors ($d_{ij} \le 1.0$~km, 1,445 links, 58.98\%):} Pairwise correlation averages $\mathbf{\bar{r} = 0.72 \pm 0.14}$. Notably, co-located multi-mast approach cameras monitoring orthogonal directions at the same physical intersection ($d_{ij} \le 50$~m, 78 links) exhibit high environmental and signal coupling ($\bar{r} = 0.84 \pm 0.08$), while distinct intersection-to-intersection arterial corridors ($50 < d_{ij} \le 1,000$~m, 1,367 links) average $\bar{r} = 0.71 \pm 0.13$.
    \item \textbf{Mid-Range Arterial Corridors ($1.0 < d_{ij} \le 2.5$~km, 736 links, 30.04\%):} Pairwise correlation attenuates to $\mathbf{\bar{r} = 0.44 \pm 0.18}$.
    \item \textbf{Transitional Network Links ($2.5 < d_{ij} \le 3.0$~km, 77 links, 3.14\%):} Pairwise correlation attenuates to $\mathbf{\bar{r} = 0.28 \pm 0.15}$.
    \item \textbf{Long-Distance Regional Corridors ($d_{ij} > 3.0$~km, 192 links, 7.84\%):} Pairwise correlation decays to $\mathbf{\bar{r} = 0.18 \pm 0.11}$.
\end{itemize}
All 2,450 valid edges are strictly accounted for ($1,445 + 736 + 77 + 192 = 2,450$). Crucially, to test whether this correlation reflects actual corridor alignment rather than general geographic proximity, we constructed an \textbf{unconnected control null model} consisting of camera pairs separated by identical distance brackets but lacking direct roadway corridor connectivity. In the proximal bracket ($d \le 1.0$~km), connected OSRM corridor pairs exhibit significantly higher correlation than unconnected control pairs ($\bar{r} = 0.72 \pm 0.14$ vs. $\bar{r}_{\text{null}} = 0.38 \pm 0.16$, Welch's $t$-test $t = 31.4, p < 10^{-15}$). Similarly, in the mid-range bracket ($1.0 < d \le 2.5$~km), connected pairs outperform unconnected controls ($\bar{r} = 0.44 \pm 0.18$ vs. $\bar{r}_{\text{null}} = 0.22 \pm 0.14$, $t = 19.8, p < 10^{-12}$). This significant quantitative margin confirms that the OSRM corridor pruning captures physical road corridor continuity beyond isotropic distance decay. Furthermore, users should note that the Gaussian RBF kernel parameter $\sigma = 1.09$~km in Equation~(2) is established mathematically from the empirical standard deviation of valid road distances ($\sigma = \text{std}(d_{ij})$) to normalize diffusion weights into $[0, 1]$, rather than serving as a direct statistical regression curve fit of empirical Pearson correlation values.

\paragraph{Corridor Directionality Cross-Validation with Longitudinal Flow Series}
To rigorously validate the physical validity of directed corridor links ($P_f, P_b$) and asymmetric edges ($1,070$ unidirectional and $238$ distance-asymmetric pairs), the derived graph was cross-validated against longitudinal 1-minute vehicle count series from the companion pilot benchmark (\textit{IC4SD-Traffic-HCM}, 86-day observation window across the identical 608 stations~\cite{le2026urban}). Evaluating the directed cross-correlation function $\text{CCF}_{ij}(\tau) = \text{Corr}(x_i(t), x_j(t + \tau))$ reveals that for valid downstream corridors $(i \to j)$, positive cross-correlation peaks at positive temporal lags $\tau^* \in [2, 6]$~minutes, consistent with actual metropolitan vehicular transit velocities ($20$--$35$~km/h across $1.0$--$2.5$~km corridors). Conversely, evaluating reverse or unconnected directions yields near-zero or non-significant lagged correlation, empirically confirming the directional fidelity of the OpenStreetMap-derived graph topology.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Observation Time Window:} The \textbf{93.6-hour} observation window captures multi-day diurnal transitions across weekdays and weekends in both the historical metropolitan core and peri-urban expansion districts (Thu Duc City, District 12, Binh Chanh, Hoc Mon, up to $10.99^\circ\text{N}$), but does not encompass longitudinal multi-month seasonal cycles (monsoon wet vs. dry season).
    \item \textbf{Snapshot Sampling vs. Continuous Video:} With a 5-minute nominal polling cadence ($\Delta T = 269.0 \pm 239.7$~s), the dataset is structured for macroscopic traffic state analysis, self-supervised representation pre-training, and spatial graph modeling, but does not support micro-level continuous vehicle trajectory tracking or optical flow.
    \item \textbf{Surveillance Web Stream Resolution:} Images are serialized at $\mathbf{512 \times 288}$ JPEG compression as delivered by the public municipal web portal, reflecting real-world public IoT camera stream conditions rather than raw uncompressed sensor feeds. While insufficient for individual vehicle OCR or facial tracking, this resolution is optimal for macroscopic density and self-supervised ViT pre-training while strictly guaranteeing statutory anonymity.
    \item \textbf{Unlabeled Bulk Corpus:} The 714,123-frame corpus is released unlabeled to facilitate self-supervised pre-training, spatio-temporal graph representation learning, and roadway background extraction without manual annotation biases.
    \item \textbf{Urban Arterial Focus and Modal Routing Generalization:} Camera stations are stationed exclusively on major urban arterials, avenues, and bridge approaches. The road graph routing distances were computed using the standardized motor-vehicle profile in OSRM because both cars and motorcycles share common directional roadways and physical lane corridors along primary arterials. However, this profile does not model dedicated motorcycle-only curbside lanes or informal cut-through routes through residential alleyways (\textit{local residential alleys and side-streets}), which lie outside the coverage of the municipal arterial camera network.
    \item \textbf{Physical Infrastructure vs. Dynamic Flow Topology:} The derived directed spatial graph models navigable roadway infrastructure connectivity and physical driving distances; it reflects static physical road geometry rather than dynamic, time-varying traffic flow propagation or real-time congestion states.
\end{enumerate}
